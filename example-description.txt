As test data, I will use figures whose behavior is known in advance.
Wikipedia provides examples of three-cell colonies and their behavior, as well as the stable Glider pattern.
These patterns should be tested on a 10x10 grid (the minimum size setting).

1. Three in a line (Blinker): The colony creates an endless oscillator alternating between horizontal and vertical lines — coordinates: (1, 1); (1, 2); (1, 3)
2. Three in a checkerboard pattern: The colony dies out — coordinates: (1, 1); (2, 2); (3, 1)
3. Two in a line, one diagonal: The colony dies out — coordinates: (1, 1); (1, 2); (3, 3)
4. Three diagonal: The colony dies out — coordinates: (1, 1); (2, 2); (3, 3)
5. Three cells in an "L-shape" (Corner): The colony turns into a stable 2x2 square (Block) and remains still — coordinates: (1, 1); (1, 2); (2, 1)
6. One cell + two in a line: The colony dies out — coordinates: (1, 1); (1, 2); (3, 1)
7. Glider: The colony loops structurally, returning to its initial orientation every 4 generations while moving diagonally until it hits a wall and stabilizes into a 2x2 block — coordinates: (1, 2); (2, 3); (3, 1); (3, 2); (3, 3)
